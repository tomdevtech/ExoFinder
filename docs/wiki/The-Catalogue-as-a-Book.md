# The catalogue as a book

ExoFinder is also a book: one PDF file with every planet, moon and dwarf planet on a page of its own. You can read it without a connection, search it, or print single pages.

## Download it

1. Open the start page of ExoFinder.
2. Scroll down to **The catalogue as a book**.
3. Click **Download full catalogue**. The file is large, because it holds several thousand pictures, so start the download where you have a good connection.

The file comes from GitHub, where ExoFinder keeps it. It is renewed whenever the catalogue changes, so a new download holds the planets that were found since.

## What is in it

| Part | What you find there |
| --- | --- |
| **Cover and foreword** | What the catalogue holds, in which order, and which pictures are real. |
| **Contents** | The solar system body by body, with the moons below their planet, and every star with the page of its first planet. Click a line to jump there. |
| **The solar system** | The Sun, the eight planets, the dwarf planets, Vesta and every known moon. |
| **Exoplanets** | Every planet of another star that NASA lists as confirmed, the nearest system first. |
| **Sources** | Where every value, map and shape comes from, with its licence. |
| **Index** | Every body by name, with its page. |

## One page per body

Every page shows the picture of the body, its name and the same values as its page on the site: size, mass and temperature, the orbit, the discovery and, for an exoplanet, its star.

- **The number** at the top right is the number of the body in the catalogue. The site shows the same number above the name, so you find a body from the book on the site and the other way round.
- **The address** at the foot of the page opens the page of the body on the site, where you can turn its model in 3D.
- **The small print** below the values says what kind of picture you see: a real map, a real shape, or an illustration.

> **Is it a photo?** The bodies of our solar system are drawn from real maps and shapes that spacecraft measured. The pictures of exoplanets are computed from their measured values, because nobody has seen one up close, and the small moons have made-up shapes. Each page says which it is.

## Find a planet in the book

- Use the search of your PDF reader (**Ctrl + F**) and type the name.
- Or open the **Index** at the end and click the name.
- The bookmarks of the file, in the side bar of your reader, follow the contents: the solar system with its moons, then star by star.
